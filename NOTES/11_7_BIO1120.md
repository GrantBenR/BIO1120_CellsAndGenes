### DNA Replication
- Watson and Crick
- "It has not escaped our notice that the specific pairing we have postulated immediately suggests a possible copying mechanism for the genetic material.
- Nature, April 25, 1953: "Molecular Structure of Nucleic Acids: A structure for deoxyribose nucleic acid.

- Each strand serves as a template from which the new complementary strand is copied
- New strands are copied from the parental strand.
- Each new double strand will have one parental strand and one new daughter strand.

- Watson and Crick's model suggested that DNA was semi-conservative
- Alternatives:
    - semi-conservative: Pairs of new and old
    - Conservative: One fully old pair, one fully new pair
    - Dispersive: Every strand is part-new and part-old

Prokaryotic DNA Replication
- The process of DNA replication is much more rapid in prokaryotes
- Circular chromosomes have a single origin of replication
- Because there are no ends ot their circular chromosomes, they do not have an "end replication problem" that needs to be solved with telomerase.

### Replication Forks
- Topoisomerase releases tortional strain as the double helix is unwound
- Helicase unqinds DNA to expose single stranded sequences
- Single strand binding protein (SSB) keeps unreplicated DNA sequences from snapping back together
- DNA polymerase adds nucleotides 5' to 3'
- RNA primase gives DNA polymerase a starting point
- DNA ligase closes gaps in phosphodiester backbone

### Prokaryotic DNA Replication
- DNA Polymerase can incorporate 2500 nucleotides per second
- E coi's 4 MB genome can be copied in less than 30 minutes and is the rate limiting step for cell diviison.
- Errors (mutations) occur at a frequency of about 1 in 1000000000 incorporated nucleotides ( 250 duplications).

- During replication, DNA polymerase identifies and corrects errors in the DNA by proofreading every newly added base.

### The end replication problem
- Because DNA synthesis is alwasys 5' to 3', linear chromosomes will become shorter every round of replication
- Telomeres add untemplated, repeated sequences onto hte ends of linear chromosomes after each round of replication.

### Mismatch repair
- If errors are not corrected during replication, the mismatch repair mechanism is employed incorrect base pairs are corrected by replacing one of the bases (ideally on the newly synthesized strand).

### Direct Reversal
- In direct reversal, common kinds of DNA dmaage (such as pyrimidine dimers induced by ultraviolet light) are repaired by dedicated enzymes.

### Excision repair
- In excision repair, uncommon kinds of DNA damage are repaired by a generalized system that recognizes distorted helices and/or stalled DNA polymerases.

### The Central Dogma
- Genetic material flows from DNA to RNA to protein
- DNA -> Transcription (RNA Polymerase) -> RNA -> Translation (Ribosomes) -> Protein
- DNA is transcribed into mRNA and carries the information necessary for protein synthesis
