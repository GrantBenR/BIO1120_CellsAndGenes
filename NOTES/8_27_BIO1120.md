### Basics
- Elements are the building blocks of all matter
- Matter is made up of atoms
- Atoms are the smallest part of an element and are composed of 3 main particles: Protons (+), Neutrons, and Electrons (-)

### Neutrons
- Subatomic particles that weigh 1.7 * 10^-24 g
- Have no charge
- Reside in atomic nuclei
### Protons
- Subatomic particles with same weight as neutron
- Reside in atomic nuclei
- Have positive charge
### Electrons
- Subatomic particles that weigh 1/2000 of the weight of other subatomic particles
- Orbit the atomic nuclei

### Atoms
- Are mostly just empty space
- Area between nucleus and orbiting electrons is often relatively large

### Elements
- Only 25 elements are essential for human life
- While there are 25 necessary, 96% of our bodies come from just 4: oxygen, carbon, nitrogen, and hydrogen
- The last 4% is from just four more elements: Phosphorus, Sulfur, Calcium, and Potassium
- Have one proton for each electron

- An element's atomic number tells us how many protons are in its atoms
- An element's mass number tells us how many protons and neutrons are in its atoms
- Atoms that have the same atomic number but different mass number are called <b>isotopes</b>
- Isotopes that have unstable nuclei which break down over time are called <b>radioactive isotopes</b>

### Hydrogen
- Has one proton and one electron
- Atomic Number: 1

### Valence
- Electrons determine the chemical behavior of an atom
- Electrons exist at energy levels that form shells around the nucleus
    - The closest shell holds up to 2 electrons
    - The second and third shell holds up to 8 electrons
- An atom is at its most stable when all of the electron positions in the outermost shell (<b>valence shell</b>) are filled.
- The number of unpaired electrons in the outermost orbital determines the properties/behaviour of an element
- This is called the <b>valence</b> of the atom 
- The Octet Rule - Atoms want to have a full valence shell of 8

### Chemical Bonds
- The chemical behavior of an atom depends on the number of electrons in its valence 
    - 1_H: 1s orbital half-full, one bond
    - 6_C: 4 Level 2 orbitals half-full, four bonds
    - 7_N: 3 level 2 orbitals half-full, three bonds
    - 8_O: 2 level 2 orbitals half-full, two bonds
- Atoms will combine to form compounds in order to reach eight electrons in their outer energy level (Octet Rule)
### Covalent bonds
- The bonds form when an electron is shared between two elements
    - <b>Nonpolar covalent bonds</b> form between two atoms of the same element or between different elements that share the electrons equally.
    - In <b>polar covalent bonds</b>, the electrons are shared unequally by the atoms.
### Hydrogen Bonds
- Interaction involving a hydrogen atom located between a pair of other atoms having a high affinity for electrons
- Example: bonds between multiple H20 molecules
### Ionic Bonds
- The bond between oppositely charged ions in a chemical compound
- Example: the positive (Na+) and negatice (cl-) charges attract. These ions stay together and form an ionic bond
- Water does a good job of dissolving salt since its polar charges pull apart the strong positive and negative charges in an ionic bond
### Van der Waals Interactions
- Molecules that are afraid of water sometimes form weak bonds with others.
