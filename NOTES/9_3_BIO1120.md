### Learning Objective:
- Recognize commonly encountered functional groups of carbon compounds, the characteristics they impart, and the names used to describe them.

### Carbon
- Forms non-polar covalent bonds with hydrogen
- Outermost shell half-full
- Each carbon atom can form four covalent bonds
    - Bonds between carbon and hydrogen and other carbon atoms are non-polar
    - Bonds between carbon and nitrogen and oxygen are polar
- Can form long chains or ring structures
- Foundation element for virtually all biomolecules

### How will these molecules these molecules behave?
- Will they interact with other molecules like themselves?
- Will they interact with water?
- Are they hydrophobic or hydrophilic

- Isomers have the same chemical formula but different structures
- Stereoisomers are compounds that are mirror images of each other
    - Like left and right gloves
    - Many drugs stereoisomers that are active and others that are not

- Functional Gorups of Carbon
    - Hydroxyl
        - Strucutural formula: -OH
        - Imparts a hyrophilic patch
        - Compounds called alcohols
        - polar
        - Examples: Methanol, Ethanol, Propanol, etc.
    - Carbonyl
        - Structural formula -Co
        - Imparts a hydrophilic patch
        - Compounds called aldehydes or ketones aldehyde names usually end with "-al"
        - Ketone names usually end with "one"
        - nonpolar
    - Carboxyl
        - Structural formula: -COOH
        - Imparts a negative charge
        - Compounds called carboxylic acids
        - Aldehyde names usually end with "-ic" acid
        - Examples: Formic acid, Acetic acid, Butyric acid, Stearic acid, etc.
        - polar
    - Amino
        - Charged, ionizes to release H+. Since carboxyl groups can release H+ ions into solution, they are considered acidic

    - Phosphate
