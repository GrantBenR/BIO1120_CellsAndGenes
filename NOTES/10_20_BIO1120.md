### Binary Fission
- Prokaryotic cells divide through a simple form of division called binary fission
- Results in a pair of daughter cells that are genetically identical to the parent cell
- Mitosis is unnecessary because there is no nucleus or multiple chromosomes

### Binary fission of a bacterium
- Bidirectional DNA replication
    - Begins at the origin of replication and moves away from the ends of the cell
- Cell elongation
    - FtsZ proteins move towards the midpoint of the cell
- Chromosome and cytoplasmic separation
    - FtsZ forms a ring around the periphery of the midpoint between the chromosomes
- Septum formation
    - this divides the cell
- Cell division
    - The cell pinches in two forming two daughter cells

### Eukaryotic chromosomes
- Genomic DNA
- Genome: all the genetic information inside a cell
    - In prokaryotes: the genome is composed of a single circular double-stranded DNA molecules
    - In eukaryotes: the genome is in several double-stranded, linear DNA molecules bound with proteins to form complexes called chromosomes
        - Human somatic cells have 46 chromosomes (23 identical pairs -> diploid)
        - Human sex cells contain one set of 23 chromosomes (haploid) called gametes.

### Chromosomes
- Matched pairs of chromosomes in a diploid organism are called homologous pairs
    - Same length
    - Have specific nucleotide segments called genes in exactly the same location
    - Control the same characteristics
    - Each one is inherited from a different planet.
- connected by centromere for reproduction, short arm is p, long arm is q

### Chromosomes
- Human somatic cells have 46 chromosomes or 23 identical pairs (diploid)
- HUman sex cells contain one set of 23 chromosomes (haploid) called gametes

- Karyotype
    - picture of all chromosomes in an individual
- cytogeneticists
    - studies chromosome genetics
### Eukaryotic cell reproduction
- An integral part of the cell cycle
- Necessary for growth repair or regeneration of tissues
- Results in genetically identical daughter cells
- A person will have about 10 quadrillion cell divisions during their lifetime

### The cell cycle
- An ordered series of events involving cell growth and cell division that produces two new daughter cells
- most cells in G1
- Phases
    - Interphase
        - G1
            - majority of cell growth and protein synthesis
        - S - DNA Synthesis
        - G2 - Cell Growth 2
    - Mitosis
    - Cytokinesis

### Mitosis
- Prophase
    - Chromosomes condense and become visible
    - Spindle fibers emerge from the centrosomes
    - Nuclear envelope breaks down
    - Centrosomes move toward opposite poles
- Prometaphase
    - Chromosomes continue ot condense
    - Kinetochores appear at the centromeres
    - Mitotic spindle microtubules attach to kinetochores
- Metaphase
    - Chromosomes are lined up at the metaphase plate
    - Each sister chromatid is attached to a spindle fiber originating from opposite poles
- Anaphase
    - Centromeres split in two
    - Sister chromatids (now called chromosomes) are plled toward opposite poles
    - Centain spindle fibers begin to elongate the cell
- Telophase
    - Chromosomes arrive at opposite poles and begin to decondense
    - Nuclear envelope material surrounds each set of chromosomes
    - The mitotic spindle breaks down
    - Spindle fibers continue to push poles apart
- Cytokinesis
    - Animal cells: a cleavage furrow separates the daughter cells
    - Plant cells: a cell plate, the precursor to a new cell wall separates the daughter cells
