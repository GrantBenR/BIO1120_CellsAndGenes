### Carbohydrates
- Made from C, H, O
- Represented by (CH2O)n where n is the number of C atoms in the molecule
- Polymer monomers: saccharides

### How much energy are in Carbohydrate covalent bonds?

- On average, are the electrons close to an atomic nucleus or far (are they high up)?
- Polar covalent bonds of water have a higher energy than nonpolar covalent bond of carbohydrates.

### Monosaccharides
- Simple sugar
- Isomers: Same chemical formula but different structurally
- Isomer examples:
    - Glucose
    - Galactose
    - Fructose

### Disaccharides
- Form when two monosaccharides undergo a dehydration reaction - glycosidic linkage
- Monomer saccharides are connected by glycosidic bonds, water is the result of the reaction
- Adding water to a dissaccharide breaks it down back into its component monosaccharides.
- Examples
    - Lactose (glucose + galactose)
    - Maltose (glucose + glucose)
    - Sucrose (glucose + fructose)

# Polysaccharides
- A long chain of monosaccharides linked by glycosidic bonds
- Examples:
    - Starch - long-term energy storage/transport
    - Glycogen - short-to-medium-term energy storage/transport
    - Cellulose - structural support in plants
    - Chitin - structural support in animal shells
