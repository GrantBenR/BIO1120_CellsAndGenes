### Learning Objectives
- Summarize the overall organization of the chemical pathways involved in photosynthesis and how these pathways accomplish the conversion of light energy into the energy of chemical bonds.
- Describe the principles of energy transformations through membrane embedded electron transport chains (ETCs).

### Overview of Photosynthesis
- Photosynthesis is the process through which organisms convert solar energy into chemical energy
- The chemical energy is used to build glucose and other carbohydrate molecules
- The energy released when these carbohydrate molecules are broken down is used to perform work, such as cellular respiration.
- Photosynthesis also releases oxygen into the atmosphere.

### Light dependent reactions
- Light dependent reactions begin in photosystems a grouping of pigment molecules and proteins.
- Photosystems exist in the membranes of thylakoids.
    1. A pigment molecule in the photosystem absorbs light energy.
    2. An electron from a chlorophyll molecule breaks free and moves to a nearby primary electron acceptor.
    3. High energy electrons travel through the electron transport chain and pump hydrogen ions into the thylakoid space.

### Generating ATP and NADPH
- Absorbed energy is stored by two types of energy carrier molecules: ATP and NADPH
- These molecules become lower energy molecules ADP and NADP+ when they release energy into the Calvin cycle

### The Calvin Cycle
- Set of chemical reactions in photosynthesis that use energy stored by light-dpeendent reactions to form glucose molecules
- The stroma of chloroplasts is the site of the reactions
- Six turns of the Calvin cycle are needed to make one glucose molecule
### Three stages of the Calvin Cycle
- Calvin cycle organized into three phases/stages:
    - Fixation
    - Reduction
    - Regeneration
