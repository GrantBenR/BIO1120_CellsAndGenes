### Reproduction
- Sexual Reproduction
    - Fusion of a haploid cell from each parent to form a single diploid cell
    - Requires two organisms of the same species - one male and one female
    - Results in variation among offspring - a potential evolutionary advantage
- Asexual Reproduction
    - Produces offspring by asexual budding, fragmentation or asexual eggs
    - Does not require another organism of the opposite sex

### Sexual life cycles
- Fertilization and meiosis alternate in sexual life cycles
- Three main categories of life cycles
    - Diploid dominant
    - Haploid-dominant
    - Alternation of generations

### Diploid dominant life cycle
- Examples: humans

### Haploid dominant life cycle
- Prominent multicellular haploid stage
- No multicellular diploid stage
- Examples: slime mold

### Alternation of generations
- Both haploid and diploid stages are apparent
- Examples: some ferns

### Meisosis
- A cell division process by which four haploid gametes are produced
- One round of chromosome duplication and two rounds of nuclear division
- Each gamete has half the number of chromosomes
- Results in nuclei that are not genetically identical
- Two rounds of division in Meiosis: Meiosis I and Meiosis II

### Phases of meiosis I 
- Meiosis is preceded by an interphase during which DNA is replicated. Cell eneters meiosis I.

### Prophase I
- Nuclear membrane breaks down
- Homologous chromosomes from both parents pair up (synapsis) and align
- Exchange of chromosome segments between non-sister homologous chromatids occurs (crossing over)
- Paired up chromosomes (two from each parent) are called tetrads
- The recombinant sister chromatid has a combination of maternal and paternal genes

### Synaptonemal Complex
- Only occurs during Prophase I of meiosis, allows recombination to occur
- maternal and paternal chromatids are lined up, compared, and "zipped" up by their transverse filaments
