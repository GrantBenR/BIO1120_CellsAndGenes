### 4 Major Classes of Biomolecules
- Carbohydrates
- Lipids
- Proteins
- Nucleic Acids

- Carbon is a central element to life because all biological macromolecules are built on a carbon framework.

### Carbohydrates
- Made from C, H, O
- Represented by (CH20)n where n is the number of C atoms in the molecule
- Polymer monomers: saccharides
- Polymer bond name: glycosidic bond
- 3 commonly encountered subtypes:
    - Monosaccharides
    - Dissaccharides

### Lipids
- Hydrophobic
- Are not true polymers
- Polar dissolves polar
- Non-polar dissolves non-polar
- Consist mostly of hydrocarbon regions
- Represented by (CH2)n where n is the number of C atoms in the molecule
- Polymer monomers: fatty acids and glycerol
- Polymer bond name: ester bonds
- Cells store energy for long-term use in the form of lipids called fats
- Fat molecule: Consists of two main components: glycerol and fatty acids.

### Fats
- Constructed from two types of smaller molecules: glycerol and fatty acids
- Glycerol is a three-carbon alcohol with a hydroxyl group attached to each carbon
- A fatty acid consists of a carboxyl group attached to a long carbon skeleton
- In a fat, three fatty acids are joined to a glycerolby an ester linkage, creating a Triacylglycerol, or triglyceride. The fatty acids in a fat can be all the same or of two or three different kinds.

### Isomers
- Compounds with the same molecular formula but different structures and properties
    - Structural isomers have different covalent arrangements of their atoms
    - Cis-trans isomers (also called geometric isomers) have the same covalent bonds but differ in their spatial arrangements.
    - Enantiomers are isomers that are mirror images of each other

### Enantiomers
- Important in the pharmaceutical industry
- Two enantiomers of a drug may have different effects
- Often only one enantiomer is biologically active
- Differering effects of enenantiomers demonstrate that organisms are sensitive to even subtle variations in molecules.

### Fatty Acids
- Vary in length (number of carbons) and in the number and locations of double bonds
- Saturated Fatty Acids: have the maximum number of hydrogen atoms possible and no double bonds
    - tend to be solid at room temperatures
- Unsaturated fatty acids: have one or more oduble bonds
    - Tend to be liquid at room temperature

### Trans fats
- Hydrogenation is the process of converting unsaturated fats to saturated fats by adding hydrogen
- Hydrogenating vegetable oils also creates unsaturated fats with trans double bonds
- These trans fats may contribute more than saturated fats to cardiovascular disease

### Purpose of fats
- The major function of fats in energy storage
- Humans and other mammals store their long-term food reserves in adipose cells
- Adipose tissue also cushions vital organs and insulates the body.

### Phospholipids
- Amphipathic (hydrophobic and hydrophillic)
- Key ocmponent of all cell membranes consists of three main components - glycerol, two fatty acids, and a phosphate group
- Form lipid bilayer sphere in water, hydrophillic end facing out, hydrophobic ends facing in

### Steriods
- Are lipids characterized by a carbon skeleton consisting of four fused rings
### Cholesterol
- A type of steroid, is a component in an animal cell membranes and a precursor from which other steroids are synthesized
- A high level are found in sex hormones: estradiol, testosterone
### Summary
- Monosaccharides serve as a major fuel for cells and raw building molecules
- Polysaccharides, polymerized form of monosaccharides, play storage and structural roles
- Lipids are a diverse group of hydrophobic molecules
    - Major function of fats is energy storage
