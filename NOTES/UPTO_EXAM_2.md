### Reticuloendothelial system
- A group of membranes and organelles in eukaryotic cells that work together to modify, package and transport lipids and proteins. Sometimes called the "endomembrane system." It includes:
    - Nuclear Envelope
    - Lysosomes
    - Vesicles
    - Endoplasmic reticulum
    - Golgi apparatus
### The nucleus and its parts
- Nucleus
    - Houses the cell's DNA in the form of chromatin
    - Directs the synthesis of ribosomes and proteins.
- Part of the nucleus
    - Nuclear envelope (double membrane)
    - Chromatin (combination of DNA and proteins)
    - Nucleolus (site of ribosome assembly)
    - Histone (protein associated with DNA)
### Endoplasmic reticulum
- Network of interconnected membranes
- Helps move substances within cells
- Two types:
    - <b>Rough endoplasmic reticulum:</b>
        - Ribosomes are attached to its cytoplasmic surface
        - Synthesizes and modifies proteins
    - <b>Smooth endpoplasmic reticulum:</b>
        - No ribosomes on its cytoplasmic surface
        - Synthesizes lipids
### Golgi apparatus
- Sorts, tags, packages, and distributes lipids and proteins
- Modified and tagged proteins are packaged into vesicles
    - Transport vesicles: Deposit their contents into other parts of the cell where they will be used
    - Secretory vesicles: Fuse with the plasma membrane and release their contents outside the cell.
- In plant cells, the Golgi apparatus synthesizes polysaccharides
### Lysosomes
- Contains digestive enzymes
- Functions:
    - Breaks down old cell organelles
    - Aids in cell renewal
    - Destroys disease-causing organisms (phagocytosis)
### Vesicles and Vacuoles
- Membrane bound sacs that function in storage and trasport
- Vacuoles:
    - Larger than vesicles
    - Membrane does not fuse with membranes of other cellular components
- Vesicles:
    - Can fuse with other membranes within cells.
### Other parts
- Ribosomes
    - protein synthesis
- Centrioles
    - Made from microtubules
    - Anchors for mitotic/meiotic spindles
- Peroxisomes
    - Oxidizes and breaks down fatty acids and amino acids, and detoxifies poisons.
### Chloroplasts (in plant cells)
- Derived from photosynthetic bacteria (endosymbiont theory)
- Have their own DNA and ribosomes
- Contain chlorophyll for photosynthesis
- Componenents:
    - intermembrane space
    - outer membrane
    - inner membrane
    - Granum (stack of thylakoids)
    - Stroma (aqeous fluid)
### Mitochondria
- Derived from free-living bacteria (endosymbiont theory)
- Have their own DNA and ribosomes
- Contain internal membranes for electron transport chain

# The fluid mosaic model

    - Polar with hydrophilic head, hydrophobic tail
- Proteins
    - Integral and peripheral proteins

### Phospholipids

- Key component of all cell membranes
- Consists of three main components- glycerol, two fatty acids, and a phosphate group

### Selectively Permeable Membranes
- Plasma membranes are selectively permeable - they allow some substances through but not others
- Passive Transport
    - No energy required for movement
    - Moves due to concentration gradient, pressure, or charge
    - Moves to equalize the gradient from high to low
### Types of passive transport
- Diffusion
    - Substances move from an area of high concentration to an area of low concentration until the concentration is equal across the space
- Facilitated transport
    - Material moves across the plasma membrane
    - Requires the assistance of transmembrane proteins
    - Moves down a concentration gradient (high to low concentration)
    - No energy is required for movement
- Osmosis
    - Special form of diffusion 
    - Diffusion of water through a semipermeable membrane
    - Water moves from an area of higher concentration to one of lower concentration or from a lower solute concentration.

Alosteric change - when a change in shape of a protein changes its function

### Tonicity
- Describe the solute in a solution
    - Measure of tonicity is called osmolarity

### Types of solutions based on tonicity
- Hypotonic
    - Solutes in cell > solutes in extracellular fluid
    - Water enters the cell
- Isotonic
    - Solutes in cell = solutes in extracellular fluid
    - no movement
- Hypertonic
    - Solutes in cell < solutes in extracellular fluid
    - Water exits the cell
### Active transport
- Movement of molecules/ions/charge
- Against the gradient
- Requires the use of energy usually from the conversion of ATP, ADP, and Pi
- Examples:
    - Electrochemical gradient
    - glucose receptor expends energy to pull molecule to itself
### Endocytosis
- Movement of large particles into cells
    - Phagocytosis
    - Pinocytosis
### Exocytosis
- Expells materials out of the cell
