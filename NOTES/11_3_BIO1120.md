### Non-Mendelian Inheritance
- Incomplete dominance
    - Neither allele is dominant
    - Heterozygous individuals have an intermediate phenotype
    - Pink snapdragon flowers - have one red alllele and one white allele
- Codominance
    - Both alleles for the same characteristic are simultaneously expressed in the heterozygote
    - Example ABO blood groups of humans.
- Multiple alleles
    - Multiple alleles may exist at the population level, such that many combinations of two alleles are observed
    - The most common phenotype or genotype in the natural population is denoted as the wild type
    - All other phenotypes or genotypes are considered variants to this type
    - Example: ABO blood type
- Sex-linked traits
    - traits (genes) located on the sex chromosomes
    - Sex chromosomes are X and Y
        - XX genotype for females
        - XY genotype for males
    - Many sex-linked recessive disorders are therefore more common in males than in females.
    - For a recessive X-linked trait to be expressed:
        - A female needs two copies of the allele (homozygous)
        - A male needs only one copy of the allele (hemizygous)
    - X-linked recessive disorders are therefore more commmon in males than in females.
- Pleiotropy
    - Single genes often have multiple effects
    - For instance, albino individuals are also often cross eyed
    - The gene that causes albinism can also cause defects in the nerve connections between eyes and the brain.
- Epistasis
    - An interaction between genes such that one gene masks or interferes with the expression of another
    - The alleles that are being masked or silenced are said to be hypostatic
    - Example: Coat color in horses
- Polygenic inheritance
    - When inheritance for a characteristic depends on the combined effects of numerous genes
    - Example: skin color in humans (at least 3 genes)
- Cytoplasmic inheritance
    - Mitochondria and chloroplasts have their own chromosomes
    - Example: variegated leaves due to random sampling of organelles during mitotic cell division
    - mitochondria are passed down maternally exclusively
- Mosaicism
    - Female mammals inactivate one of their two X chromosomes (Barr bodies) early in embryonic development
    - Example: calico cats are usually females with X chromosomes that code for two different coat colors.
