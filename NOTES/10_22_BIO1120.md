### Interphase
- Chromosomes are diffuse difficult to see
- Nucleolus is visible
- Nuclear envelope is intact
### Prophase
- Chromosomes condense and become visible
- Spindle fibers emerge from the centromeres
- Nuclear envelope breaks apart
- Nucleolus becomes less distinct
### Metaphase
- Chromosomes are lined up at a metaphase plate
- Each sister chromatid is attached to a spindle fiber originating from opposite poles
- Chromosomes are most condensed.
### Anaphase
- Centromeres come apart
- Sister chromatids (now called chromosomes) are pulled toward opposite poles
- Chromosomes become less visible
### Telophase
- Chromosomes arrive at opposite poles
- Nuclear envelope redevelops
- The mitotic spindle breaks down
- Nucleolus begins to reappear
### Cytokinesis
- Cleavage of a cell into two halves

### Growth phases
- G0 phase
    - Cell is in a quiescent stage
    - Some cells enter G0 temporarily until an external singal triggers the onset of G1
    - Some cells rarely divide and remain in G0 permanently
        - Mature cardiac muscle, nerve cells

### Cell Cycle Checkpoints
- 3 Major Checkpoints
    - G1 checkpoint
        - Restriction point
        - Determines whether all conditions are favorable for cell division to proceed
        - Checks for damage to the genomic DNA
    - G2 checkpoint
        - Ensures proper chromosome duplication
    - M checkpoint
        - Spindle checkpoint
        - Occurs near the end of the metaphase stage of mitosis
        - Determines if all the sister chromatids are correctly attached to the spindle microtubules

### Knudson's two-hit hypothesis
- Proto-oncogenes
    - Genes that code for the positive cell-cycle regulators
    - When mutated, they become oncogenes
    - Example: Cdk
- Tumor Suppressor Genes
    - Genes that code for the negative regulator proteins
    - When activated, they prevent the cell from undergoing cell division
    - If mutated, the negative regulator might not be able to halt the cell cycle if there is a problem
    - Example: p53

### Loss of cell cycle control
- Cancer and the Cell Cycle
    - Before a cell divides the DNA is checked to ensure it has replicated correctly
    - Cancer is a disease caused by uncontrolled cell division
    - Uncontrolled growth of mutated cells can outpase the growth of normal cells resulting in a tumor

### Reproduction
- Sexual reproduction
    - Fusion of a haploid clel from each parent to form a single diploid cell
    - Requires two organisms of the same species - one male and one female
    - Results in variation among offspring - a potential evolutionary advantage
- Asexual Reproduction
    - Produces offspring by asexual budding, fragmentation, or asexual eggs
    - Does not require another organism of the opposite sex

- The rearrangement/shuffling of genes in sexual reproduction allows natural selection to work. Asexual reproduction requires mutation for any changes to happen.
